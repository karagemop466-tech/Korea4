🧮 How a day is classified

  • Explicit games first: every Westwood One NFL broadcast for the rest of the 2026 season (from their official schedule), the 49ers' remaining 2026 games, Stanford/Cal's remaining 2026 games, the CFP bracket dates, and the Super Bowls are placed at their real times with average durations.
  • Then verified season rules: the officially released 2027 MLB calendar (spring training Feb 19–Mar 23; season Mar 25–Sep 26; All-Star dark days Jul 12–14) is shown as a “typical slate window” by weekday — e.g., a Saturday has games somewhere in MLB from ~10:05 AM to ~10:00 PM PT. First-pitch times for 2027 are mostly TBD, so the window is labeled ESTIMATE even though the dates are officially released.
  • 2028/2029: no official MLB/NFL/ACC schedules exist. Windows follow the verified 2026/2027 patterns (Opening Day = last Thursday of March; NFL kickoff = Thursday after Labor Day; etc.) and are labeled ESTIMATE everywhere.
  • “light day” = ≤3.5 hours total on air (e.g., a lone Monday Night Football). “Free” = zero minutes.

Model range: Nov 2, 2026 – Dec 31, 2029. Full source list and methodology: research-notes.md in the repo; open items on the Flagged for Review page.

Kickoff/first-pitch times can be flexed by leagues & networks on 6–12 days' notice (esp. NCAAF) — confirm close to the day. Windows use average lengths: MLB 2:38 (MLB official, 2025) · NFL ~3:10 · NCAAF 3:26.
