Longest free runs (vacation answer)

How many consecutive days in 2027, 2028 and 2029 have no published live-radio sports game, ranked by evidence strength.

Snapshot, not a live feed. Data captured 2026-11-03T23:27:13+00:00. Schedules, broadcast carriage and actual game lengths can change; some sports and future schedules are missing. Gaps are planning estimates, not verified free time. Check the review flags and original sources before booking.

Answer

No 7-, 14- or 21-day stretch of consecutive days in 2027, 2028 or 2029 is free under the strict reading (no published MLB, NFL, Stanford/Cal, Westwood One or captured NBA/NHL game, and no officially anchored all-day event).

The longest run of consecutive days with no published game of any monitored category is 5 days (2027: 4, 2028: 5, 2029: 5), but every one of those runs exists because a schedule has not been released, so it is not evidence that no game is on.

The longest gap bracketed by two published official games is 4 days (2027-01-19 → 2027-01-22). That is the honest answer to "how long can I go away".

What the data supports: early winter (January to mid-February) repeatedly produces two-to-four day gaps between a published Sharks game and the next published NFL playoff weekend, because baseball is out of season and college football has ended. The longest such gap bracketed by two published official games is listed at the top of the ranked table. From late February to early October, at least one MLB game is published nearly every day, so no gap longer than a day or two can exist even in the model.

No 7-, 14- or 21-day stretch of consecutive days inside 2027-2029 is free under the strict reading. The longest runs with no published game are short early-winter gaps (see shortlist); every longer empty stretch is empty only because the 2027 NFL/WWO, 2028 and 2029 schedules have not been released, so it cannot be called free. Official NHL game rows for the Sharks included as busy days: 88 (2026-09-20 → 2027-04-10). NBA/Warriors game rows are still missing (the official NBA CDN refuses datacenter requests); flagged as F-021.

Definition used on this page

A day is FREE only when the snapshot contains no block of any class for it. A day with a published game, an officially anchored all-day event of unknown start time, or only the released-season model does not count as free. This is stricter than asking whether some minutes are free.

Day classMeaning
PUBLISHED_OFFICIALAt least one published official game row (official start + estimated duration, official network window, or official date with TBD start).
UNVERIFIED_ANCHORNo published time, but an officially anchored event occupies the whole day (e.g. Super Bowl, CFP championship, dated college game).
MODEL_ONLYOnly the clearly-labelled 364-day model of unreleased seasons. Still treated as a candidate block.
NO_BLOCKNo block at all in the snapshot.

Why a long summer window cannot exist

This is the core reason the answer is days, not weeks. Counting only published official game rows, MLB plays on almost every calendar day of its season, so nearly every summer day is busy no matter which club is involved. The days without a game are the All-Star break and the day before Opening Night — exactly the short gaps ranked below.
