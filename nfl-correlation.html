Method (G6 standard). Two binary legs A and B with correlation ρ. Bernoulli covariance: P(A∩B) = P(A)·P(B) + ρ·√(P(A)(1−P(A))·P(B)(1−P(B))), clamped to the Fréchet bounds [max(0, P(A)+P(B)−1), min(P(A), P(B))]. Same-game parlays are capped at 2 legs under the current gates; a 3-leg approximation is provided for research only. Pearson correlation on game-level season totals from nflverse play-by-play (mirror of official NFL feeds), weeks 1-10. Game-level correlations are NOT the correlation of specific prop legs against a line; they are a descriptive starting point and must be re-estimated against actual prop lines before any parlay use.

Why correlation decides parlay value

A 2-leg parlay of independent 60% legs pays as a 36% event. With ρ = +0.30 the joint probability is ≈ 45% — the same ticket is worth very different amounts depending on correlation. This is why G6 forces every multi-leg registration to declare its correlation source, and why the same-game cap exists: positive correlation is usually purchased at full price in real markets, so unadjusted independence math overstates value. No odds are ingested, so this page never claims a ticket is +EV — it computes probabilities only.
