SG-05 · Model transparency panel on every originated line

PROPOSED · Suggested P1 · site · Impact: High · Effort: Medium

Problem: A fair line is only actionable if the reader can see its inputs, their official sources and the sensitivity of the number to each assumption.

Evidence: The originated lines depend on a Pythagorean exponent, an assumed margin standard deviation, a home-edge assumption, a rest coefficient and an availability penalty table; the published line moves when any of them moves.

Options considered
  1. A. Expandable inputs panel per line with the sensitivity of the line to each parameter.
  2. B. A single methodology page (current behaviour).
  3. C. Publish only the final number (rejected: it hides the assumptions that the gates are supposed to police).

Recommendation: Option A, including a sensitivity line such as 'line moves to X if the margin standard deviation is 10 instead of 12'.

Decision needed before implementation: Confirm which parameters must be exposed and whether the sensitivity view is a table or a chart.

Manual-review sources: https://www.nfl.com/standings/league/2026/REG · https://www.nfl.com/schedules/2026/REG1

SG-10 · A price source, or an explicit decision to run the program without one

RECOMMENDED · Suggested P1 · data · Impact: High · Effort: Low for A, High for B

Problem: Edge, ROI and leaderboard points cannot be computed without a price, and no official source publishes NFL betting lines, so the program currently originates fair values only.

Evidence: The NFL publishes schedules, results, injury reports and standings on nfl.com; none of the official pages carries a price. Every gate that scores a strategy (G2, G5, G9, G12) is blocked on this.

Options considered
  1. A. Connect the program's own bet log, in which the owner records the price and timestamp of every bet taken.
  2. B. Connect a licensed price feed.
  3. C. Run the program as a pure line-origination and analysis system with no scoring.
  4. D. Infer prices from third-party pages (rejected: not an official source and not reproducible).

Recommendation: Option A or C. A is strictly better because it makes every gate testable with the owner's own records; C is the honest fallback if no bet log exists.

Decision needed before implementation: Choose between A, B and C. This single choice unlocks or permanently blocks the scoring half of the program.

SG-13 · Publish the luck baseline next to every measured statistic

RECOMMENDED · Suggested P1 · model · Impact: High · Effort: Low

Problem: A measured effect is meaningless without its noise floor, and the available historical panel shows limited season-to-season persistence while the game-level model leaves substantial margin variance unexplained.

Evidence: On the official 2024–2025 completed-season standings panel (32 team pairs), points differential per game has year-to-year r = 0.32, r² = 0.10. Separately, the exploratory game-level model leaves 79.5% of margin variance unexplained. Neither statistic is a causal partition of betting skill versus luck.

Options considered
  1. A. Publish the baseline statistic beside every effect size and require the effect to exceed it.
  2. B. Keep the baseline on a separate analysis page.
  3. C. Skip the baseline (rejected: it is the difference between a strategy and a coin flip).

Recommendation: Option A. Every published effect should carry the number it has to beat, in the same row.

Decision needed before implementation: Confirm whether the luck baseline should be shown as a percentage or as a required margin.
