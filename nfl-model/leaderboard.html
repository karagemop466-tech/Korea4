Leaderboard competition design

A season-long internal competition where strategies earn their place by gate evidence and closing-line edge, not by which month they got lucky in.

A season-long internal competition in which independently specified strategies compete for limited funding. Ranking is on evidence of information advantage and discipline, not on realised profit, because at NFL sample sizes profit is mostly noise. Nothing here is a wagering service: entries are internal, free to join, and rewards are not a share of a stake pool.

Tiers

Promotion between tiers is by gate evidence, and a strategy can be demoted by a failed gate but never by a bad month.

Discipline rules

The rules that keep a ranked competition from becoming a leaderboard of whoever logged the most flattering subset.

  • A submission is a strategy id plus a version hash; the version is frozen at entry and any change restarts its sample clock. Re-entering a strategy with a new threshold to buy another lottery ticket is prohibited and the extra entries still count in the multiple-comparison pool.
  • Bets must be recorded before the registered cut-off with the price at that moment; a bet recorded after kickoff is a note, never a data point, and late entries are published as violations.
  • No deletion of losing entries. Withdrawal removes a strategy from future contention only.
  • Every entrant publishes the same three numbers for every result row: n, standard error, and the share of PnL that variance could plausibly explain.
  • The ledger is hash-chained (each entry includes the digest of the previous one) so that history cannot be quietly rewritten; this mirrors how the repository already stores raw official responses with SHA-256 hashes.

Reward structure (awaiting decision)

Status: AWAITING OWNER DECISION (Q-15, SG-14). Recommendation: R-A for season one; R-B only after entries stay internal; R-C not before two seasons of gate-passing evidence exists.

R-A Recognition + infrastructure
Winner gets the funding allocation, priority on compute/data spend, and a permanent place in the core; no cash prize.
Risk: Low - nothing resembles a stake pool or a prize competition.
R-B Credits
Non-cash credits (data subscription months, cloud compute, a season of the price feed) awarded on the weighted score.
Risk: Low, but it is a thing of value: if third parties can enter, a legal read on prize-competition rules in the owner's jurisdiction is needed first.
R-C Cash from the operating budget
Fixed cash amounts set before the season, paid from the owner's budget, never from entrants' stakes.
Risk: Highest - touches prize-competition and gambling-adjacent law; requires counsel (Q-13).
