Results columns, explained

Every strategy panel repeats the same nine result fields. They are blank in every panel, and the reason is a data gap, not an omission of work.

Result columns are blank by design. Any number placed there must come from a stored, timestamped, settled bet ledger whose inputs satisfy gates G-01..G-04; filling them by estimate is what this catalogue exists to prevent.

benchmark · all

FieldSpecification
HypothesisRandom selection at the market's prices loses the vig; every candidate strategy must beat this, not break even.
Signal rule (mechanical)No signal. Bet the same game and side as each candidate entry but at the closing price, with no model input.
Required inputsclosing prices; settlement
Correlation treatmentnone (single leg)

ST-01 · Our-line versus close: model-disagreement edge SPEC-NOT-TESTED

single · spread, total, moneyline

FieldSpecification
HypothesisIf our originated number is better calibrated than the market on specific contexts, the discrepancy is the edge; if it is not, the discrepancy is noise - and the market close tells us which.
Signal rule (mechanical)Take the side when (our no-vig probability for that side) - (market no-vig probability at the cut-off) >= threshold, threshold fixed in the pre-registration; skip otherwise. Score primarily on closing-line value, not on wins.
Required inputsour model line; market open and close; vig removal; settlement
Correlation treatmentsingle leg
Stakingfractional Kelly, cap 0.5% bankroll per bet, sized on the modelled edge not the observed one
Why the edge could existModel information the market may underweight in specific contexts, measured against the close. It is the only framework where 'the model was right' is separable from 'the team won'.
Why it probably failsPublic closing lines are extremely informative; a decade-old academic comparison found opening and closing lines statistically similar in predictive value, i.e. little to harvest in the general case.

ST-02 · Total-first origination, then derive the spread SPEC-NOT-TESTED

single · total -> spread

FieldSpecification
HypothesisThe total and the margin come from the same scoring process; pricing the total first and deriving the spread enforces internal consistency and exposes whichever market is off.
Signal rule (mechanical)Model expected points per possession for both teams -> expected total and expected margin; compare each to the market; trade the market with the larger standardised discrepancy, and never trade both against each other on the same game in the same cycle.
Required inputspossession-level scoring model; venue/rest adjustments; prices for total and spread
Correlation treatmenttwo views of one game - the legs must not be combined into a parlay without a joint model (see the correlation page)
Stakingfractional Kelly at the larger edge, halved when both markets disagree
Why the edge could existInternal consistency: the market prices the two legs with separate panels and separate flows. Structural, not narrative: it is a no-arbitrage-ish relation rather than an opinion about a team.
Why it probably failsIf our scoring model is biased, both markets are 'disagreeing' with our bias and the largest discrepancy is simply our worst error.
Gates appliedG-01, G-02, G-03, G-04, G-05, G-07, G-09, G-10, G-16
Data gapRequires our own model (R-07) and prices (R-02).

ST-03 · Key-number value on half-point purchases SPEC-NOT-TESTED

single · spread (alternate lines)

FieldSpecification
HypothesisNFL margins pile up on 3 and 7, so moving a line across a key number has a step value that vig does not always cover; buying a half point is sometimes free and sometimes a rip-off.
Signal rule (mechanical)Compute win probability at each candidate line from our discrete margin distribution; act only when the value of the extra half point exceeds its vig cost by the registered margin, and only on the side of the key number where our model puts material mass.
Required inputsdiscrete margin distribution (SG-08); alternate line prices; settlement
Correlation treatmentsingle leg
Stakingflat unit; sizing increases only after 3 seasons of stability
Why the edge could existA pricing asymmetry in the vig schedule rather than in opinions about teams. It is arithmetic on the margin distribution; the distribution is estimable from official box scores alone, with no purchase required.
Why it probably failsPublic knowledge of key numbers means the vig schedule already prices it; also our margin distribution could be wrong where it matters most.

ST-04 · Starting-QB availability shock SPEC-NOT-TESTED

single · spread, total, moneyline

FieldSpecification
HypothesisA confirmed change at the most important position moves true win probability more than the line adjusts in the time available, especially when the news lands after the bulk of the market's action.
Signal rule (mechanical)Trade only on the official report states (a QB listed Did Not Participate or Out/Doubtful, or a starter removed from a report he was on), against the price at the moment of the report, with a maximum allowed age of the information; no reporter rumours, no aggregate-site claims.
Required inputsofficial report history per report day; tick-level prices after the report; QB usage/quality baseline; settlement
Correlation treatmentsingle leg per game; if two legs from the same game are wanted, they must be priced jointly
Stakingfractional Kelly on the estimated shift, hard cap 1% of bankroll, no martingale chasing
Why the edge could existSpeed plus the asymmetry that a practice report is public while its interpretation is not. It is one of the few inputs where the market must reprice a hard fact rather than an opinion.
Why it probably failsBooks reprice instantly, and the official report is deliberately coarse: a certain-to-play player is not listed in the game status at all, so 'absence' is not news, which is verified league policy, not a workaround.
Gates appliedG-01, G-02, G-03, G-04, G-05, G-06, G-09, G-10, G-12, G-13, G-16

ST-05 · Defensive-backfield depletion SPEC-NOT-TESTED

single · team total, spread, opponent WR props

FieldSpecification
HypothesisCornerback depth is thin by roster construction; losing two top-cornerback bodies changes coverage quality more than a single-count 'out' list implies, and the line prices players, not depth charts.
Signal rule (mechanical)Deficiency index = sum over the secondary of (starter weight x availability penalty), where availability comes from the official report and the club depth chart's listed depth order; act when the index crosses a registered threshold and the opposing team's pass-market moves less than the index-implied shift.
Required inputsofficial report per day; club depth charts (32 clubs); per-position win-fraud baseline from our own model; prices
Correlation treatmentthe team-total leg and the WR-prop leg in the same game are positively dependent by construction; combining them requires the joint model
Stakingfractional Kelly, 0.5% cap
Why the edge could existDepth-chart information that the line does not read directly, on the exact requirement in the brief about second and third-string replacements. Positional asymmetry is real: a team can list one or two corners at all, which the fetched 49ers chart demonstrates (left cornerback has a single name on the chart).
Why it probably failsCoordinator adjustments (more safety rotation, more press from a nickel) can absorb the loss; the market may already know the practice report and price it.

ST-06 · Offensive-line depletion against the run game SPEC-NOT-TESTED

single · opposing RB carries, team rush attempts, under on opponent pass attempts

FieldSpecification
HypothesisLine-play availability shifts play distribution (more rushes, shorter downs) before it shifts scoring, so usage markets reprice later than side markets.
Signal rule (mechanical)Count unavailable interior linemen weighted by chart depth (a first-string guard and his only listed backup out is a different state from one reserve out); when the index crosses the threshold, take the usage side that our own projection model says is lagging, never the narrative side.
Required inputsofficial report; depth charts; prior-season usage baselines; prop prices; settlement definitions for carries/attempt counting
Correlation treatmentmultiple usage legs on the same team are strongly positively dependent; do not ticket them without the joint model
Staking0.25% cap; props are low-liquidity and vig-heavy
Why the edge could existUsage-versus-scoring repricing lag on a market with fewer participants. Prop books have thinner limits and fewer dedicated bettors than the sides market, so the same information moves prices less.
Why it probably failsCarry-counting and 'attempt' definitions are settled by the stats standard, not by our reading; a definitional mismatch turns a right bet into a lost ticket (Q-12).

ST-07 · First-time-starter snap-share repricing SPEC-NOT-TESTED

prop-combo · player receptions, rush attempts, fantasy-score props

FieldSpecification
HypothesisWhen a replacement becomes a full-time participant, his usage jumps immediately while the line is still anchored to his historical per-game average, because props are set off volume expectations rather than snap projections.
Signal rule (mechanical)Detect a depth-chart promotion (position 1 changes between consecutive official club charts) or a DNP-by-a-starter pattern; take the OVER on the promoted player's volume prop when our projection of his snaps times per-snap production exceeds the line plus vig cost.
Required inputstwo consecutive club depth charts; official report; per-snap production baseline; prop lines
Correlation treatmentreceptions and receiving yards for the same player are highly positively dependent and must be priced jointly, not multiplied
Staking0.25% cap, never both legs of a combo unless the joint price beats the operator's
Why the edge could existA lag between roster reality and the prop-setting anchor. It uses only inputs that are public and dated, so it can be reconstructed honestly for past seasons if the charts are archived going forward.
Why it probably failsSnap-share data for the projection baseline is official only the day after games (verified league documentation), so the baseline itself is stale by construction; and prop books may already shade for the backup.

ST-08 · Short-week and travel context on totals SPEC-NOT-TESTED

single · total, first-half total

FieldSpecification
HypothesisReduced preparation time and long resets suppress scoring pace, and the effect is more reliable than the market's uniform treatment of a short week.
Signal rule (mechanical)Only for the documented context states (Thursday game following a Sunday game, or an international-series game in the following week), act when our context-adjusted total differs from the market total by the registered amount and the market has not moved by the cut-off.
Required inputsofficial schedule with kickoff and TV slots; venue list; rest-day computation; prices
Correlation treatmentsingle leg; a game-total leg and a first-half-total leg from the same game must not be parlayed without a joint model
Staking0.5% cap
Why the edge could existA context term with a small, stable effect and few bettors specialising in it. The repo already holds an official 2026 schedule with prime-time slots and international rows, so the context features need no new purchase; the effect is measurable from box scores alone.
Why it probably failsThe effect is small, so it needs an enormous sample (a 1-2 point shift on a total is a fraction of a percent of win probability), and this is the classic place where a season of luck masquerades as an edge.

ST-09 · Availability-driven alternate-line consistency SPEC-NOT-TESTED

single · alternate spreads and team totals

FieldSpecification
HypothesisAlternate lines are set by formula from the main line, so a large availability change can leave an internal inconsistency (a team-total alternate and its spread alternate implying different margins) rather than an opinion error.
Signal rule (mechanical)Compute the implied margin from the alternate spread and from the alternate team totals; when the implied difference exceeds the vig-cost-adjusted bound, take the inconsistent leg. Purely internal-arithmetic based.
Required inputsalternate line prices across the ladder; our availability delta
Correlation treatmentlegs from the same ladder are nested, so their correlation is exact by construction (see correlation page) and they must never be parlayed together
Staking0.25% cap; expect limits and voids on ladder bets
Why the edge could existMechanical pricing of a ladder that is repriced less often than the main line. It is checkable arithmetic, not a forecast; the failure mode is mostly 'the ladder was never really inconsistent'.
Why it probably failsOperators restrict ladder parlays and shrink limits precisely here; and a 'no-action' rule on a ladders market can void the winning leg.
Gates appliedG-01, G-02, G-04, G-05, G-09, G-10, G-12, G-13, G-16
Data gapNeeds ladder prices, which the documented vendor's market list does not include - a separate source question.

ST-10 · Structurally correlated SGP (team total + same-team skill position) SPEC-NOT-TESTED

parlay · same-game parlay

FieldSpecification
HypothesisSome same-game leg pairs have dependence we can model well because both are driven by one latent variable (expected plays and efficiency for that team); operators price this with a correlation assumption we may be able to beat.
Signal rule (mechanical)Price the joint with our own two-dimensional model; enter only when our joint probability exceeds the operator's implied probability after removing vig, AND the pair's correlation is measured on our own history with an interval that excludes the operator's assumption.
Required inputsjoint model; SGP prices and the operator's correlation/repricing rules; our own resolved ticket history for the correlation
Correlation treatmentthe whole strategy is the correlation: an unmeasured pair is not eligible (Q-20)
Staking0.25% cap, and never more than one SGP per game in the ledger
Why the edge could existBetter dependence modelling than the default assumption in the boost. Boosts are marketing: a boosted price can still be worse than fair, and knowing the real dependence is the way to tell.
Why it probably failsIt cannot be tested in year one at all - it requires our own resolved tickets; and if the operator voids correlated combos, the ticket never settles as modelled (Q-07).
Gates appliedG-01, G-02, G-03, G-04, G-05, G-07, G-09, G-10, G-11, G-12, G-13, G-16
Data gapEverything: joint model, SGP price capture, and a measured correlation.

ST-11 · Cross-game parlays with an explicit independence penalty SPEC-NOT-TESTED

parlay · multi-game parlay

FieldSpecification
HypothesisCross-game parlay pricing assumes independence; games in the same week share league-wide information (rules, weather patterns, divisional scouting), so a modest dependence assumption should usually be enough to find the ticket that is not overpriced.
Signal rule (mechanical)Price the ticket as the product of marginal probabilities times an explicit dependence factor measured from our own resolved tickets (or set conservatively to 1.0 with the ticket then flagged as unverified-dependence); require the operator's implied probability to fall short of ours by the registered margin.
Required inputsmarginal probabilities; prices per leg; resolved-ticket dependence estimate; operator parlay rules
Correlation treatmentdependence factor is the input; unmeasured means the ticket is logged but not funded
Staking0.25% cap per ticket; total parlay exposure capped at 3% of bankroll
Why the edge could existThe vig stack on parlays is only worth attacking when the legs are genuinely near-independent and each leg is a real bet. A parlay of individually-value legs is a legitimate way to reduce per-bet cost if and only if the joint price does not double-charge vig.
Why it probably failsMost operators price the same legs worse in a parlay than as singles; if any leg pushes, non-SGP parlay rules can leave the ticket settled short, which changes the distribution we assumed (published in the operator rules we cite).
Gates appliedG-01, G-02, G-04, G-05, G-07, G-08, G-09, G-10, G-11, G-16
Data gapMarginal prices (R-02) and the dependence measurement (needs resolved history).

ST-12 · Same-player prop combo priced from one latent distribution SPEC-NOT-TESTED

prop-combo · receptions + receiving yards, rushing yards + rushing TDs

FieldSpecification
HypothesisTwo props on the same player are the same random variable viewed twice; pricing them from a single fitted distribution exposes combos the operator priced as if independent, and equally exposes the ones that are far worse.
Signal rule (mechanical)Model per-player volume with a truncated count distribution and yards-per-catch noise; enumerate the offered points; enter a combo only when the joint probability from our distribution exceeds the operator's implied probability after devigging, with the same distribution also required to reproduce each single-prop price within tolerance (self-consistency check).
Required inputsplayer projections; per-prop single lines and prices; combo prices; snap baseline; stat definitions
Correlation treatmentnear-deterministic dependence; the correlation is the trade, and it must be declared with our fitted value and interval
Staking0.25% cap; one combo per player per game
Why the edge could existA structural mispricing in how combos are built, not a forecast about a player. Self-consistency between the singles and the combo is a falsifiable arithmetic condition, so the strategy tells us when it is wrong, not just when it wins.
Why it probably failsIf our per-player distribution is wrong in its tail, both the singles and the combo are wrong together and the self-consistency check passes while the bet loses.
Gates appliedG-01, G-02, G-03, G-04, G-05, G-09, G-10, G-11, G-12, G-13, G-16
Data gapProp lines and the projection baseline (Q-11, Q-12).

ST-13 · Model-vs-market disagreement with CLV-only promotion SPEC-NOT-TESTED

meta · all

FieldSpecification
HypothesisThe promotion decision itself should be taken on closing-line value, because CLV is the only quantity in the system that measures whether we were better informed than the market rather than luckier than it.
Signal rule (mechanical)Score every candidate strategy by mean (our probability minus market-implied probability) at entry, and its t-statistic on the CLV distribution, with no use of game outcomes at all for promotion; outcomes are used only to confirm calibration and detect a broken settlement path.
Required inputsentry prices; closing prices; our probabilities; settlement (for calibration only)
Correlation treatmentnot applicable - it is a ranking rule, and it is the reason the leaderboard exists
Stakingnot a staking rule; a gate on staking
Why the edge could existMethodological: it removes the outcome lottery from the funding decision. Widely used in professional practice because it needs a much smaller sample than PnL to detect a real edge.
Why it probably failsCLV is not the same as profit: a strategy can beat the close consistently and still lose on a market that is right for reasons outside our model; it also presumes we can capture the close, which is a data question with a cost.
Gates appliedG-05, G-06, G-08, G-10, G-14, G-15, G-17
