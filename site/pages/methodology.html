Methodology — what counts as "free"

Every minute is free only when none of the monitored broadcasts are on Bay Area AM/FM. This page defines the exact rule, time zone, durations, and estimation method.

1) The free-time rule

A minute is free when zero of these are on live AM/FM radio receivable in Outer Sunset 94122: All 30 MLB clubs (preseason, regular season, All-Star, postseason, special games like Mexico City/Field of Dreams) • NFL — all 32 teams, preseason + regular + postseason (49ers high priority) • Westwood One Sports national NFL radio (TNF/SNF/MNF + playoffs + Super Bowl) • NCAAF — Stanford and California Golden Bears (high priority).
Busy = at least one of those is on-air live (including pregame/halftime). We use a broadcast window: game duration + pregame + halftime where applicable. If you miss the start you miss the game — we count the whole window as busy.
Free = none of those on-air. You can turn the radio off. Example intraday: if games are 1:05–3:35 PM and 8:15–11:30 PM, you're free 6:00 AM–1:05 PM, 3:35–8:15 PM, after 11:30 PM (PST).

2) Time zone — PST/PDT (America/Los_Angeles)

All dates and times on this site are shown in PT — i.e., PST (UTC-8) in winter, PDT (UTC-7) in summer. We label it “PST” per your request but automatically use daylight when applicable (Mar–Nov). Example: MLB Opening Night Mar 25 5:05 PM PT = 5:05 PM PDT (summer). NFL Sep games are PDT; Dec-Feb games are PST. The calendar uses Intl.DateTimeFormat with America/Los_Angeles so DST shifts are handled.

Why PST? You live in 94122 — we show the time you'd see on your radio clock.

4) Vacation window definition

Free day = zero games at any time that calendar day (PST) from any monitored league. Free window = consecutive free days. We search for 7-day, 14-day, and 21-day windows in each calendar year (Jan 1–Dec 31). 2026 is verified; 2027-2029 are estimated and labeled as such everywhere.

Why 21-day never appears: MLB runs 185 days (Mar 25–Sep 27) with only a 3-day All-Star gap. NFL wraps around it (Sep–Feb) + spring training (Feb 24 onward) + bowl season (Dec). Together they leave only ~14 free days post-Super Bowl. Math: 365 - ~260 busy days = ~105 days, but they're not consecutive. Longest consecutive is post-Super Bowl → spring training.

If you relax the rule to “ignore MLB spring training” (preseason), you get 35-42 days (Feb 9 → Mar 24) — noted as an alternate view.

5) Estimation method for 2027-2029

No official schedules are released for 2027-2029 as of Nov 2026 (MLB releases ~July prior year, NFL ~May prior year, ACC ~Jan prior year). We estimate using 15-year historical patterns:

  • MLB: Opening Day = last Thursday in March (Mar 26 in 2026 → Mar 25 2027 (Thu), Mar 30 2028 (Thu, leap year + Olympics buffer), Mar 29 2029). All-Star = second Tue in July (Jul 13-14). Regular end = last Sun Sep (Sep 27 ±2). Verified pattern: MLB 2026 release
  • NFL: Kickoff = Thu after Labor Day (Sep 10 2026 in Melbourne → Sep 9 2027, Sep 7 2028, Sep 6 2029). Super Bowl = 1st or 2nd Sun Feb; SoFi gets LXI (Feb 7 2027 est).
  • NCAAF: Week 0 ≈ Aug 28, Week 1 = Sat after Labor Day. Big Game (Stanford@Cal) = Sat before Thanksgiving (Nov 21 2026 → Nov 20 2027 etc.) Cal 2026 via Write for California

Every estimated date is marked estimated and never shown as verified. Tables leave time blank where unknown; flagged items go to Flagged for Review. When officials release, we replace.

6) What we count as "Westwood One"

Westwood One Sports is the official NFL network audio partner (39th consecutive season). It carries every primetime regular-season game (Thursday Night, Sunday Night, Monday Night), plus all playoff games and Super Bowl, on ~500 affiliates. In SF, that's KGO 810 AM (and KNBR simulcast). This means even if the 49ers are on bye, a Thursday/Monday national game still makes your night busy per your definition — we count those.

Source: westwoodonesports.com/nfl-schedule • Wikipedia — NFL on Westwood One • Inside Radio — Wild Card coverage
